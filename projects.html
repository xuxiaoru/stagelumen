Case Study 01 — Outdoor Festival

Open-Air Electronic Festival — Three Stages, Twelve Days

Germany’s summer festival season does not wait for good weather. The promoter wanted a main-stage rig that could stay rigged and powered through twelve days of rain instead of being bagged and struck every night — without doubling the generator budget.

Region Germany Year 2025 Venue 3 stages · main 24 m × 14 m Fixtures 168 Run 12 show days

The brief

  • Weather exposure. Nine of the twelve show days had rain. Every fixture on the three exposed trusses had to run uncovered, unattended, overnight.
  • Load-in window. The site gave 36 hours to rig three stages. Conventional bagging and de-rigging each night was not an option.
  • Power budget. Two 125 A generators fed the whole site. The rig had to be re-specified around a hard 60 kW ceiling per stage.
  • Fault visibility. With 168 fixtures spread over 200 m of field, the crew needed to find a dead unit from FOH instead of walking the site.

What we delivered

  • IP65 where it matters. SL-IP-B400, SL-IP-W400 and SL-IP-H600 bodies with sealed optics and conformal-coated drivers on all exposed positions; standard bodies only under the stage roof.
  • Generator load model. We delivered a per-position current draw table so the site electrician could balance phases before load-in, not during it.
  • RDM monitoring. Two SL-C2 controllers distributed Art-Net and polled every fixture — fixture status, temperature and hours visible from FOH.
  • Pre-rigged trusses. Fixtures shipped clamped and cabled on pre-built truss sections in flight cases, cutting the rig time per stage to about 12 hours.

Case Study 02 — Arena Tour

Arena Tour — 30-Date European Leg

A 3-in-1 hybrid platform let the designer hold beam, spot and wash looks across eight weeks of venues without changing the fixture count on the truss — or the truck pack.

Region 9 countries, Europe Year 2025 Venue Arenas, 8,000–15,000 cap Fixtures 151 Run 30 shows / 8 weeks

The brief

  • Variable trims. Trim height ranged from 9 m to 16 m between venues. Fixed-beam rigs either punched through or died in the air.
  • Truck pack limits. Two 13.6 m trailers for the whole show. Every additional fixture type meant another case and another case meant another truck.
  • Colour consistency. Fixtures from three production batches had to match on camera across the whole leg.
  • Spares strategy. A failure in week seven could not end the tour. The spares kit had to be defined before load-out, not after.

What we delivered

  • One platform, three roles. SL-H400 hybrids on the mid truss covered beam, gobo and wash, removing a whole fixture type from the pack.
  • Batch binning report. All 151 units shipped from a single LED bin with a measured colour report, so replacement units matched on sight.
  • Unified firmware. Every fixture left on identical firmware with a single personality file, so a swap on the road needed no re-patching.
  • Defined spares kit. A 10% allocation with a case map — the crew knew which case to open at 2 a.m. in a foreign arena.

Case Study 03 — Rental Fleet

Regional Rental House — Fleet Standardisation

Over two seasons we replaced four mixed-brand generations of moving lights with six StageLumen families — and cut the spare-parts catalogue from 38 SKUs to nine.

Region Australia Year 2024–2025 Venue Sydney + Melbourne warehouses Fixtures 324 Run 2 phases

The brief

  • Four generations of stock. Beam, spot and wash came from three different brands bought between 2015 and 2022. Nothing shared a spare part or a personality.
  • Spare-parts overhead. 38 active spare SKUs tied up working capital and shelf space for parts that moved twice a year.
  • Prep time. Every fixture type had its own prep routine. Technicians were retrained per brand, and prep errors went out on trucks.
  • Colour matching. Mixed bins meant a truck pack from two different years never matched on site.

What we delivered

  • Common internals. PSU, main board, fan and LED module are shared across the SL-B family — one spare set covers three models.
  • Six families, not sixteen. The fleet consolidated onto B, H, W, PAR, pixel and control families covering every job type the house books.
  • Technician training. Two on-site sessions covering service, firmware update and RDM workflow for the whole workshop team.
  • RDM inventory workflow. Fixture hours and fault logs now drive the service schedule instead of a clipboard.

Case Study 04 — Theatre Refit

Municipal Theatre — Tungsten to LED Refit

A 620-seat civic theatre moved off a 1990s tungsten inventory in six weeks. The brief was not brightness — it was silence, dimmer-rack removal and skin tones the resident companies would accept.

Region United Kingdom Year 2024 Venue 620 seats · 14 m proscenium Fixtures 52 Run 6-week install

The brief

  • Noise floor. FOH bars sit 6 m from the front row. Anything above ~28 dBA was audible during quiet scenes.
  • Skin tones. Resident opera and drama companies rejected previous LED trials on R9. The refit had to satisfy the lighting designers, not the spreadsheet.
  • Existing infrastructure. Six dimmed circuits per position. Re-cabling the grid was not in the budget.
  • Fixed install window. Six weeks between the summer closure and the first rehearsal, including focusing.

What we delivered

  • Fanless fresnels. SL-F200 and SL-F300 run convection-cooled — measured 24 dBA at the FOH bar, below the room’s own noise floor.
  • Photometric study first. We modelled the 14 m stage width for even coverage before a single fixture shipped, and sized the FOH positions from the lux plot.
  • Reuse of existing wiring. New DMX distribution ran alongside the old dimmed circuits, so the grid was not re-cabled.
  • Focus and handover. Two days on site for focusing, preset writing and a handover session with the venue technicians.

Case Study 05 — Club & DJ

Nightclub Relaunch — Pixel-First Room Design

A 700 m² main room with a 3.4 m ceiling. Long-throw movers were never going to work, so the room was designed around pixel surfaces, short-throw wash and two laser systems.

Region Spain Year 2025 Venue 700 m² main room · 3.4 m trim Fixtures 92 Run 3-week fit-out

The brief

  • Ceiling height. At 3.4 m trim a 2° beam has nowhere to go. The room needed coverage and movement, not throw.
  • Heat load. The venue’s existing AC could not absorb another 30 kW of lighting on a full room.
  • Different every night. The operator wanted twelve distinct looks pre-built, so a Tuesday resident and a Saturday guest DJ do not look identical.
  • Cable paths. No accessible ceiling void over the dance floor — wireless and battery fixtures were the only option for wall positions.

What we delivered

  • Pixel-first layout. A ceiling grid of SL-P1000 bars plus an SL-P1500 façade behind the booth gives the room its movement without any long-throw fixture.
  • Wireless uplighting. SL-PARWAUV battery units on the perimeter with wireless DMX — no cable runs, no holes in a listed building.
  • Twelve programmed looks. Looks, chases and timecode triggers were pre-programmed and handed over on the SL-C2, with a written cue sheet.
  • Thermal budget. Total installed draw held at 18 kW, inside the existing AC capacity.

Case Study 06 — Church & Worship

Multi-Site Church — Broadcast-Ready Sanctuary Package

Four campuses, one standard package. The lighting had to hold skin tones at 5600K for the camera while staying bright enough for congregational singing — and be operable by volunteers.

Region United States Year 2025 Venue 4 campuses · 900–1,800 seats Fixtures 59 Run 4-campus rollout

The brief

  • Camera first. The stream is the front door. Skin tones had to hold at 5600K with no green shift and no flicker at 60 fps and 120 fps.
  • Volunteer operation. The Sunday operator is a volunteer with ten minutes of training. Presets had to be one button, not a cue stack.
  • Congregational light. House light for singing has to be bright enough to read a book without washing out the stage.
  • Replication. The same package had to drop into four rooms of different sizes without a redesign each time.

What we delivered

  • Standard package. One fixture schedule repeated across all four campuses, with per-room quantity scaling from the photometric study.
  • One-button presets. Six scenes written into each SL-C1: preaching, worship, baptisms, communion, stream-only and clean-up.
  • Flicker-free drivers. High-frequency PWM verified at 60/120/240 fps before shipment, with the test report in the delivery pack.
  • Per-campus spares. Each campus holds its own spare kit and a laminated quick-start card next to the console.

How a StageLumen Project Runs

From first brief to trained operators — the six stages every project follows, with a named deliverable at each one.

Brief & site data

You send room dimensions, trim height, photos, power availability and show format. We tell you within 48 hours what else we need.

Specification & quotation

Fixture schedule, BOM and a photometric study for your room — not a generic package. Quotation follows the specification, not the other way around.

Sample & approval

Demo units or a sample batch where the order justifies it. IES / LDT files, DMX charts and the colour binning report are signed off before production.

Production & QC

48-hour burn-in on every unit, individual QC report, firmware flashed to the agreed build. Batch photos and serial list before shipment.

Logistics & documentation

Packing list, commercial invoice, CE / ETL files, manuals and customs paperwork. EXW, FOB, CIF or DDP.

Commissioning & handover

On-site or remote commissioning, operator training, recommended spare-parts kit and a 24-month warranty from commissioning date.
