Building a Rental-House Moving Head Inventory: Scaling from 10 to 60 Fixtures

Past ten fixtures, a rental fleet stops being a shopping list and becomes a utilisation problem. How to tier it, spare it and buy it in quantity.

September 14, 2026 · RiGeBa Lighting Team · How-To

The first ten fixtures are a shopping list. The next fifty are an inventory, and they behave differently: some units earn their keep every week, some sit in cases for a month, and the difference between the two is the only number that should drive the next purchase.

The short answer

Grow a rental fleet in tiers, not one fixture at a time. Fill the middle tier first — the US$150 to US$220 units that cover the most requests per dollar — then add depth to whichever tier is booking out. Carry roughly ten percent spares once the fleet passes twenty units, buy in quantity to reach the published volume tier, and add IP65 or CMY only when a specific job you have already quoted asks for it.

What breaks when a fleet grows past ten fixtures?

Three things stop working at once, and most rental houses notice them in this order.

What breaksSymptomThe fix
UtilisationHalf the fleet sits idle while a few units are always outTrack which tier books out; buy depth there
StandardisationFour fixture families, four menu structures, four spare-part binsConsolidate onto one chassis family per tier
Failure rateA dead fixture is now a lost job, not an inconvenienceA spare ratio of about 10%
Case logisticsMixed fixture sizes mean the truck never packs efficientlyBuy to a case size, not to a fixture count

The utilisation problem is the one that costs the most money, because it is invisible. Below ten fixtures, every job uses everything you own and every purchase looks justified. At forty fixtures, the units booking out every week are subsidising the ones that go out twice a year, and the only way to see which is which is to log it.

How many spares should the fleet carry?

Roughly ten percent, rounded up, once the fleet is over twenty units. Below that, two spares is usually enough, because a failure can be covered by pulling a fixture from a non-critical position.

Spares only need to cover the workhorse tier. A premium fixture going down the day before a load-out is a phone call to another rental house; a workhorse going down is the difference between shipping the job and not.

When should the fleet add IP65 or CMY?

When a quoted job asks for it, not before. Both roughly double the cost of a position, and both sit idle unless the work is genuinely there.

Zoom is the exception. It costs about 30 percent more and removes the constraint that otherwise forces two different wash angles into the inventory, so it pays for itself in fixture count rather than in day rate.

Our IP65 moving heads — the 230W outdoor beam and the IP65 zoom wash — are the two units rental houses add first, because they cover the two outdoor requests that come up most: an aerial beam for a festival stage and a wash for a courtyard or marquee.

What should the fleet not buy next?

More variety. The instinct at forty fixtures is to add a fourth family to cover a request that came up once. That adds a spare-part bin, a case size and a menu structure for one job a year. Sub-hire that job and buy more of what is already booking out.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changes once a rental fleet passes ten fixtures?

Three things: utilisation becomes measurable, standardisation starts saving real money, and the fleet needs a spare ratio. Below ten fixtures every job uses everything you own. Above it, some fixtures sit idle and you can see which ones earn their keep.

How many spares should a rental house carry?

About ten percent, rounded up, once the fleet is over twenty units. A fleet of 40 working fixtures wants four spares. Below twenty, two spares covers it, because the failure probability is low enough that a swap from a non-critical position works.

Does buying in quantity actually save money?

Between 3 and 16 percent depending on the model, and the discount is published rather than negotiated. Our volume tiers start at 20 to 50 units and are listed on each product page, so the saving is known before you ask.

Should a rental fleet be standardised on one fixture?

On one family, not one model. Standardising on a single chassis means one set of spare parts, one set of flight cases and one menu structure to learn, while still covering wash, beam and hybrid duties from the same platform.

When is it worth adding IP65 fixtures to a rental fleet?

When you turn down outdoor work more than twice a season. IP65 units cost roughly twice the indoor equivalent, so the break-even is a handful of booked outdoor jobs per year, not a hypothetical one.
