Live streaming has become an essential part of many industries, including entertainment, education, and business. When it comes to lighting a live-streamed set, there are several key considerations to keep in mind, including colour temperature, flicker, and camera-safe dimming.

## What is colour temperature and why is it important for live streaming?
Colour temperature refers to the warmth or coolness of a light source, measured in Kelvin (K). For live streaming, a colour temperature of 3200K is often preferred as it provides a warm and natural look. However, this can vary depending on the specific requirements of the production. 

## How do I choose the right colour temperature for my live stream?
When choosing a colour temperature for your live stream, consider the mood or atmosphere you want to create. Warm colour temperatures (2700K-3000K) are often used to create a cozy or intimate atmosphere, while cool colour temperatures (5000K-6500K) are often used to create a bright or energetic atmosphere. 

## What is flicker and how can I avoid it in my live stream?
Flicker refers to the visible flashing or pulsing of a light source, which can be distracting and unprofessional in a live stream. To avoid flicker, use lighting fixtures with a high frequency or pulse-width modulation (PWM) to reduce the visibility of flicker. 

## How do I ensure camera-safe dimming in my live stream?
Camera-safe dimming refers to the ability of a lighting fixture to dim smoothly and without flicker, making it suitable for use in live streaming and video production. This is typically achieved through the use of advanced dimming technologies such as PWM or DMX control. 

## What are the key considerations when choosing a lighting fixture for live streaming?
When choosing a lighting fixture for live streaming, consider factors such as colour temperature, CRI, and dimming capabilities. It's also essential to think about the specific requirements of your production, such as the size of the set, the number of cameras, and the desired mood or atmosphere. 

## How do I determine the right size and configuration for my lighting fixtures?
To determine the right size and configuration for your lighting fixtures, consider the size of the set and the number of cameras being used. A larger set may require more powerful lighting fixtures, while a smaller set may require less powerful fixtures. 

## What is the difference between DMX and PWM dimming?
DMX (Digital Multiplex) and PWM (Pulse-Width Modulation) are two different dimming technologies used in lighting fixtures. DMX is a digital protocol that allows for precise control over lighting fixtures, while PWM is an analog technology that uses a high-frequency pulse to dim the light. 

## Can I use LED lighting fixtures for live streaming?
Yes, LED lighting fixtures can be used for live streaming. In fact, LEDs are often preferred for live streaming due to their high CRI, energy efficiency, and camera-safe dimming capabilities. 

## How do I control my lighting fixtures during a live stream?
Lighting fixtures can be controlled using a variety of methods, including DMX, PWM, and manual controls. For live streaming, it's often best to use a DMX controller, which allows for precise control over the lighting fixtures and can be programmed to create complex lighting scenes. 

## What are some common mistakes to avoid when lighting a live-streamed set?
Some common mistakes to avoid when lighting a live-streamed set include using lighting fixtures with low CRI, failing to consider the colour temperature of the fixtures, and not using camera-safe dimming.
