Live streaming has become an essential part of many industries, including entertainment, education, and business. One crucial aspect of live streaming is lighting, as it can greatly impact the quality and atmosphere of the video. For live-streamed sets, it's essential to consider colour temperature, flicker, and camera-safe dimming to ensure a high-quality video.

## The short answer
For a live-streamed set, use a light with a colour temperature of 3200K or 5600K, such as the 300W LED Panel light -warm, which has a colour temperature of 3200K/5600K and a CRI of Ra≥90.

## What colour temperature is best for live streaming?
The best colour temperature for live streaming depends on the desired atmosphere. For a warm and cozy atmosphere, a colour temperature of 3200K is suitable, while 5600K is better for a cool and modern look. The 300W LED Panel light -warm, for example, has a colour temperature of 3200K/5600K, making it a versatile option for different live streaming applications.

## How can I avoid flicker in my live stream?
To avoid flicker, use lights with a high refresh rate, such as those with a 10KHz dimming refresh PWM strobe. This will ensure a smooth and flicker-free video. The RGWS100A-36P3W, for instance, features a flicker-free 10KHz dimming refresh PWM strobe, making it an excellent choice for live streaming.

## What is the importance of camera-safe dimming?
Camera-safe dimming is crucial to prevent flicker and ensure a smooth video. It allows the lights to be dimmed without affecting the camera's frame rate, resulting in a high-quality video. Look for lights with a camera-safe dimming feature, such as the RG-ST112A-S20f336A, which has a linear dimming feature from 0-100%.

Using a high CRI light for live streaming can greatly improve the colour accuracy and overall quality of the video. ## What are the common mistakes to avoid when lighting a live-streamed set?
Common mistakes to avoid when lighting a live-streamed set include:
* Using lights with a low CRI, which can result in poor colour accuracy
* Not considering the colour temperature of the lights, which can affect the overall atmosphere of the video
* Not using camera-safe dimming, which can result in flicker and a poor-quality video
* Not positioning the lights correctly, which can result in harsh shadows and poor lighting
