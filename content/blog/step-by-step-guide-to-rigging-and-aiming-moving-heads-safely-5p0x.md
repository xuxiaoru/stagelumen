Most rigging failures are not dramatic. A clamp is tightened on the wrong tube size, a safety bond is clipped to the truss chord next to the clamp instead of to the structure, or a fixture is hung and then nobody checks whether the truss underneath it was rated for the load. Each of those is a thirty-second mistake.

## The short answer

Work in one order and never skip a step: check the structure and its rated load, fix the primary clamp to the correct tube, attach a secondary safety bond to the structure, connect power and data with a strain relief, set the DMX address, then aim at the widest beam angle. Hardware is cheap compared with a fixture — a clamp is a few dollars against several hundred for the unit hanging from it.

## Step 1: is the structure rated for the load?

Before anything is hung, the supporting structure has to be adequate. This is the step most often assumed rather than checked.

| Check | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Structure rating | Point load capacity of the truss or beam, not just the span rating |
| Total load | Every fixture, clamp, cable and bond on that point, added up |
| Dynamic load | Moving heads shift their weight as they pan — the load is not static |
| Fixing method | The truss or beam is itself properly suspended or ground-supported |
| Access | How the fixture will be reached for focusing and service |

A [double hook clamp](/products/truss/dg-ls52f-400) is the right choice for a heavy moving head: two hooks share the load and the fixture cannot rotate off a single point. The single hook version suits lighter fixtures and bars.

Two rules that catch the common errors. The clamp must be the correct size for the tube it grips — a 50 mm clamp on a 60 mm chord does not seat properly and will slip. And the clamp must be tightened to the point where the fixture cannot be moved by hand, then checked again after the first hour of movement.

## Step 3: does every fixture need a secondary suspension?

Every fixture hung above people needs a safety bond, and the bond has to go to the structure — not to the truss chord beside the clamp, and not to another fixture.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Bond to the structure, not the chord | A chord can fail with the clamp |
| Use a rated steel bond or cable | Zip ties and rope are not secondary suspension |
| Give it slack so it is not load-bearing | It should catch, not carry |
| One bond per fixture | Not one bond for a bar of four |
| Inspect before every use | A frayed bond is a replaced bond |

## Step 4: power and data

Connect last, dress properly, and strain-relieve everything.

| Task | Detail |
|---|---|
| Power off before connecting | Always — including before patching data |
| Strain relief | Cable tied to the yoke or the clamp, never hanging from the connector |
| Drip loop outdoors | Lowest point below the connector |
| DMX termination | A 120 Ω terminator at the end of the last run |
| Cable routing | Away from the pan/tilt arc — a snagged cable is a broken fixture |
| Test before the room fills | Power every fixture and check it homes correctly |

A moving head sweeps a large volume. Anything loose in that volume — a cable, a bond, a case lid — will be found by the fixture at speed.

## Step 5: how should the address and mode be set?

Address before aiming, because the fixture has to respond before it can be pointed.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Choose the channel mode | Fewer channels for effects, 16-bit for slow moves |
| Write the plan down | Address, position and mode per fixture, taped to the case |
| Set the address on the fixture | Verify on the display, not on the console |
| Confirm the fixture homes | Home position tells you the motors are working |
| Terminate the run | Prevents the intermittent faults that waste an hour |

## Step 6: how should the fixture be aimed?

Open the beam fully before focusing. A fixture focused at its narrowest setting is wrong at every other setting, and the mistake is invisible until the show.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Set the widest beam | Zoom and iris fully open |
| Drive pan and tilt from a console | Bring the pool to the mark slowly |
| Aim from the fixture's final position | Where it sits in the rig, not on a bench |
| Check the extremes | Run the full pan and tilt range and look for collisions |
| Mark the position | A paint mark on the yoke saves re-focusing |
| Record the value | The console position, not just the physical mark |

## What is the inspection routine?

| Interval | Check |
|---|---|
| Before every use (touring) | Clamp tightness, bond condition, cable strain relief |
| Monthly (permanent) | Visual check of hardware, any sign of corrosion |
| Annually (permanent) | Full inspection: clamps, bonds, structure, cable condition |
| After any transport | Every clamp and bond on the fixture that moved |
| After any modification | Re-check load and re-terminate data |
