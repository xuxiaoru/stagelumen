Moving Head FAQ: Lifespan, Service, Spare Parts and What to Ask Your Supplier

The LED is rarely what fails. What actually ends a moving head's life, which spares to stock, and the questions to put to a supplier.

September 20, 2026 · RiGeBa Lighting Team · How-To

The LED engine is almost never the part that fails. That surprises buyers, because every fixture is sold on LED life, and it matters because the parts that do fail are cheap, replaceable, and worth asking about before an order rather than after.

The short answer

Expect the LED to outlast everything around it. Budget for fans, belts and driver boards instead, stock one spare fixture per ten units, and make spare-part availability a condition of the order. Ask the supplier six specific questions before paying a deposit — the answers tell you more about the real cost of ownership than any specification sheet.

What actually fails, and when?

Heat and movement. Both are unavoidable in a moving head, and both concentrate on the parts that are easiest to overlook.

The pattern is that everything on the list is heat-related. A fixture kept clean and cool outlives the same fixture run hot in a dusty ceiling void by years, which is why maintenance does more for lifespan than specification.

Is it worth repairing or replacing?

It depends on where the fixture sits in the price ladder, because the repair cost is roughly constant while the replacement cost is not.

What six questions should you ask a supplier?

These six answers predict the cost of ownership better than any specification.

QuestionWhy it mattersA weak answer
How long are spare parts guaranteed available?Determines whether a repair is possible at all"We will try"
What are the warranty terms, in writing?Verbal assurances do not survive a claimA number with no scope
Is the LED engine field-replaceable?Turns a dead fixture into a parts swap"It is sealed"
What fan is fitted, and is it a stock size?A proprietary fan means a proprietary repairNo specification given
What does the service documentation cover?Decides whether in-house repair is realistic"Contact us"
Can a sample be tested before the production order?The only real check on any of the aboveSample refused

The last question is the one that separates a supplier with confidence from one without it. A production order placed without testing a sample is a bet on every claim in the quotation.

What should a maintenance schedule look like?

Little and often. Most of the work is cleaning, and cleaning is what protects the fans.

IntervalTaskWhy
MonthlyVacuum intake filters and fan grillesDust is what kills fans and capacitors

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an LED moving head last?

The LED engine is usually the last thing to go — rated lives are typically tens of thousands of hours. What ends a fixture's service life earlier is a fan bearing, a drive belt, a stepper motor or an electrolytic capacitor in the driver, all of which are moving or heat-stressed parts.

What fails first on a moving head?

Fans, in practice. They run continuously, they pull dust through the housing, and a seized fan cooks the driver board. The second most common failure is a belt or a pan/tilt motor on fixtures that move hard every night.

Is it worth repairing a cheap moving head?

Below about US$150 per unit, usually not, unless the failure is a fan or a belt. Above US$250, repair is almost always cheaper than replacement, provided the spare part is available. Ask about part availability before you buy, not after.

Which spare parts should a venue stock?

Fans, belts and a power supply for each fixture family in the rig, plus one complete spare fixture per ten units. Gobos, lenses and yoke covers are worth holding for fixtures that move nightly.

What should I ask a supplier before ordering?

Six things: spare-part availability and for how long, the warranty terms in writing, whether the LED engine is field-replaceable, the fan specification, what the service documentation covers, and whether a sample can be tested before the production order.
