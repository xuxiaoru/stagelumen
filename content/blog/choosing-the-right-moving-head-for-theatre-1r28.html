Choosing the Right Moving Head for Theatre

Noise, colour temperature and a clean beam edge matter more than output in a theatre. What to specify, with EXW prices from US$64 to US$223.

September 17, 2026 · RiGeBa Lighting Team · How-To

Theatre is the one application where the loudest specification is usually the wrong one. A fixture that is bright, fast and fully featured will still be rejected if the audience can hear it, if it cannot hold a warm white, or if its edge is soft where the lighting designer needs a cut.

The short answer

Specify three things before output: noise, colour temperature and edge quality. Ask for a tunable white range of roughly 3200K to 5600K, a cooling design that is inaudible at the throw distance, and a beam angle quoted in degrees with lux at your distance. In our range a tunable 2×100W wash starts at US$64 and a zoom profile with a clean edge at US$168.

What actually matters in a theatre fixture?

Four criteria, in this order. Output comes last because theatrical throws are short and brightness is rarely the limiting factor.

CriterionWhat to ask forWhy it decides the purchase
NoiseStated dB at 1 m, and the cooling methodAudible fixture noise ruins quiet scenes
Colour temperatureTunable 3200K-5600KFixed 6500K reads cold on skin and cannot be corrected
Edge qualityHard edge with cutters, or a profile lensFront light must be kept off the audience
Beam angleDegrees, plus lux at your throw distanceThe only figure that predicts coverage
OutputWattage and LED configurationRarely the constraint in a theatre

A specification that lists output first and says nothing about noise is a specification written by someone who has not sat in the front row.

What colour temperature should I choose?

Tunable is almost always the right answer, because the same fixture then covers a warm interior and a cool exterior without a gel. Fixed 3200K is acceptable when the room only ever does warm, naturalistic work; fixed 6500K is only right for broadcast-neutral or deliberately cold looks.

The test: look at a face under the fixture at the colour temperature you intend to use, and compare it against the room's ambient. Theatre skin tones are decided by the ambient wash far more than by the key light.

Do I need moving heads or profiles?

Front light is a profile job. A 200W zoom profile at US$168 throws a hard, cuttable edge that keeps light off the audience, and it does not move during a scene. Moving heads belong in a theatre rig where positions have to change between shows, or where the movement itself is the effect — not in the front truss.

How should the rig be focused and maintained?

Theatre rigs are judged on consistency between performances, which makes maintenance part of the design rather than an afterthought.

  • Log the focus for every position — pan, tilt and zoom — so a knocked fixture can be restored without a designer in the room.
  • Clean fan filters on a schedule. A dust-loaded filter raises the fan speed, and the fan is the noise source you paid to avoid.
  • Track LED hours per fixture, because output between two fixtures of the same model diverges over time and a rig that was even on opening night may not be at closing.
  • Replace gaskets and fan filters as consumables rather than waiting for failure.
  • Re-check focus after any repatch or trim change, before the next performance, not during it.

A theatre rig with a maintained focus log can be re-lit by a technician in an afternoon. The same rig without one needs the original designer, which is a more expensive kind of maintenance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quiet does a theatre fixture need to be?

Quiet enough that a person in the front row cannot hear it during a scene. Cooling design matters more than the fan brand: a fixture that moves air through a large, slow fan or by natural convection will be far quieter than one using a small high-speed fan, at the same wattage.

What colour temperature should theatre fixtures be?

Tunable between roughly 3200K and 5600K, so the same fixture covers a warm interior scene and a cool exterior one. A fixed 6500K fixture is the wrong default for theatre, because it reads cold against skin and cannot be pulled back.

Is a beam angle of 10R meaningful?

No. 10R is a discharge lamp designation, not a beam angle. Ask for the beam angle in degrees and the lux at the throw distance you will use, and ignore lamp-family labels in a specification.

Do I need moving heads or profiles for theatre front light?

Profiles, unless the front positions have to change between shows. A profile gives a hard, cuttable edge that keeps light off the audience, which is the requirement a moving head only meets at extra cost and with extra noise.

How much output does a small theatre need?

Less than most people expect, because the throw is short. A 2x100W tunable white wash at US$64 covers a small stage front truss; the money is better spent on an even rig than on output.
