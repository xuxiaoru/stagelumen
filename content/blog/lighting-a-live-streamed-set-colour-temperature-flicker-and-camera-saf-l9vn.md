Live streaming has become a popular way to reach audiences, and proper lighting is essential for a high-quality stream. Lighting a live-streamed set requires careful consideration of colour temperature, flicker, and camera-safe dimming. 

## The short answer
Use a colour temperature of 3200K, such as the 300W LED Panel light -warm white and cold white 2in1, to provide a natural and flattering light for the subject.

## What is the ideal colour temperature for live streaming?
The ideal colour temperature for live streaming is between 3200K and 5600K, as it provides a natural and flattering light for the subject. The 300W LED Panel light -warm white and cold white 2in1 is a good option, with a colour temperature of 3200K/5600K/2-in-1 optional.

## How do I choose the right colour temperature for my live stream?
To choose the right colour temperature for your live stream, consider the subject and the atmosphere you want to create. A warmer colour temperature, such as 3200K, can create a cozy and intimate atmosphere, while a cooler colour temperature, such as 5600K, can create a bright and energetic atmosphere.

## What is flicker and how can I avoid it?
Flicker is a phenomenon that occurs when the light source is not synchronized with the camera's frame rate, causing a stuttering or flickering effect. To avoid flicker, use lights with a high frequency or a flicker-free mode. The RG-ST112A-S20f336A RGB LED 1008 Colouring Strobe Light has a high brightness LED5050SMD light source, which can help reduce flicker.

## How do I achieve camera-safe dimming?
Camera-safe dimming refers to the ability to dim lights without causing flicker or other issues that can affect the camera's ability to capture a smooth and stable image. The RG-ST1000B-S30f1000A LED RGB 3In1 1000W Colouring Strobe has a linear dimming function, which allows for smooth and camera-safe dimming.

## What are the key considerations when choosing lights for live streaming?
When choosing lights for live streaming, consider the colour temperature, brightness, and dimming capabilities. You should also consider the size and weight of the lights, as well as their power consumption and control modes.

## How do I position my lights for live streaming?
