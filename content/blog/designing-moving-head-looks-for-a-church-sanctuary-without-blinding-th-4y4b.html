Designing Moving-Head Looks for a Church Sanctuary without Blinding the Congregation

Select a moving-head fixture with a beam angle of 10 degrees or less to avoid direct glare on the congregation.

September 28, 2026 · RiGeBa Lighting Team · How-To

Choosing the Right Moving-Head Fixture

When designing moving-head looks for a church sanctuary, it's essential to consider the congregation's comfort and safety. Direct glare from moving-head fixtures can be distracting and even cause eye strain. To avoid this, select a moving-head fixture with a beam angle of 10 degrees or less.

Wattage Considerations

The wattage of a moving-head fixture affects its brightness and heat output. For a church sanctuary, a lower wattage fixture (around 60-100W) is often sufficient to provide a subtle, ambient light. Higher wattage fixtures (200-300W) can be used for more dramatic effects, but be cautious not to overpower the space.

Beam Angle and Control Protocol

A narrow beam angle (10 degrees or less) is crucial for avoiding direct glare on the congregation. Consider fixtures with a beam angle of 5-10 degrees for a more focused light. Control protocol is also essential, as it determines how the fixture responds to commands. Popular control protocols include DMX 512 and self-walking modes.

IP Rating and Outdoor Use

If the moving-head fixtures will be used outdoors or in areas with high humidity, ensure they have an IP65 rating or higher. This will protect the fixture from dust and water ingress. For example, the RG-ML420WC-KCH93 has an IP65 rating, making it suitable for outdoor use.

Practical Trade-Offs

When selecting moving-head fixtures, consider the following trade-offs:

  • Beam angle vs. brightness: A narrower beam angle may result in a less bright light, but it's essential for avoiding direct glare.
  • Wattage vs. heat output: Higher wattage fixtures produce more heat, which can be a concern in a church sanctuary.
  • Control protocol vs. flexibility: Self-walking modes and DMX 512 offer more flexibility in terms of control, but may require additional equipment or programming.

Fixture Selection

Based on the criteria outlined above, consider the following fixtures:

  • The New 295W 10R beam moving head light (USD 332) has a narrow beam angle of 10 degrees and a moderate wattage of 295W.
  • The 6W Moving head full color laser (USD 375) has a beam angle of 540 degrees (X axis) and 270 degrees (Y axis), making it suitable for more dramatic effects.
  • The 6 heads full-color swinging laser arrows moving head laser (USD 358) has a beam angle of 240 degrees (Y axis) and can swing in the X axis, offering a unique effect.

Conclusion

Designing moving-head looks for a church sanctuary requires careful consideration of the congregation's comfort and safety. By selecting fixtures with a narrow beam angle, moderate wattage, and suitable control protocol, you can create a visually appealing and comfortable environment for your congregation. When selecting fixtures, weigh the trade-offs between beam angle, wattage, and control protocol to find the best solution for your needs.
