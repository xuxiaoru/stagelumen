DMX vs Art-Net vs sACN: which control protocol to specify for a touring rig

Specify DMX for a 10-head moving head with a 10,000 lumen output

September 26, 2026 · RiGeBa Lighting Team · How-To

Introduction

When it comes to specifying a control protocol for a touring rig, the choice can be overwhelming. DMX, Art-Net, and sACN are three popular options, each with its own strengths and weaknesses. In this article, we will delve into the details of each protocol and provide a head-to-head comparison to help you make an informed decision.

DMX Protocol

DMX is a widely used protocol for controlling lighting fixtures. It is a linear protocol that uses a single cable to transmit data between devices. DMX is commonly used in touring rigs and is supported by most lighting consoles.

Art-Net Protocol

Art-Net is a network protocol that allows for the transmission of data between devices over a network. It is commonly used in live events and installations. Art-Net is known for its reliability and flexibility, but it requires a network infrastructure to function.

sACN Protocol

sACN is a protocol that is similar to Art-Net, but it is more widely supported and has a lower latency. sACN is commonly used in touring rigs and is known for its reliability and flexibility.

Comparison

ProtocolDMXArt-NetsACN
ReliabilityHighHighHigh
FlexibilityLowHighHigh
LatencyHighMediumLow
Network InfrastructureNoYesNo
CostLowMediumHigh
ComplexityLowMediumHigh

Conclusion

In conclusion, the choice of control protocol depends on the specific needs of your touring rig. DMX is a good choice for touring rigs that require a simple and reliable solution, while Art-Net and sACN offer more flexibility and reliability for live events and installations.

Next Steps

Before making a final decision, consider the following factors:

  • The type of lighting fixtures being used
  • The size and complexity of the touring rig
  • The required level of reliability and flexibility
  • The budget for the control protocol

DMX Protocol in Practice

DMX is commonly used in touring rigs for its simplicity and reliability. For example, a 10-head moving head with a 10,000 lumen output can be controlled using DMX. The DMX protocol allows for precise control of each head, making it ideal for touring rigs that require high-quality lighting.

Art-Net Protocol in Practice

Art-Net is commonly used in live events and installations for its reliability and flexibility. For example, a 5-head LED wall can be controlled using Art-Net. The Art-Net protocol allows for the transmission of data between devices over a network, making it ideal for large-scale events and installations.

sACN Protocol in Practice

sACN is commonly used in touring rigs for its reliability and flexibility. For example, a 10-head moving head with a 10,000 lumen output can be controlled using sACN. The sACN protocol has a lower latency than DMX and Art-Net, making it ideal for touring rigs that require high-speed control.

  • The type of lighting fixtures being used
  • The size and complexity of the touring rig
  • The required level of reliability and flexibility
  • The budget for the control protocol
