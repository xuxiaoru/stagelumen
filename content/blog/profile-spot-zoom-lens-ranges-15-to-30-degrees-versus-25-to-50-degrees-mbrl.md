## What is the difference between a 15-30 degree and 25-50 degree beam angle?
A 15-30 degree beam angle is typically used for longer throws and more precise control, while a 25-50 degree beam angle is used for shorter throws and a wider coverage area. The choice of beam angle depends on the specific application and the desired effect.

## What are the advantages of a 15-30 degree beam angle?
A 15-30 degree beam angle provides a more precise and controlled light beam, making it ideal for applications where a narrow beam is required. This beam angle range is also suitable for longer throws, as it can maintain its intensity over a greater distance.

## What are the disadvantages of a 15-30 degree beam angle?
One of the main disadvantages of a 15-30 degree beam angle is that it may not provide enough coverage for wider areas. This can result in the need for multiple lights to achieve the desired effect, which can increase the overall cost and complexity of the lighting system.

## What are the advantages of a 25-50 degree beam angle?
A 25-50 degree beam angle provides a wider coverage area, making it ideal for applications where a broader beam is required. This beam angle range is also suitable for shorter throws, as it can provide a more even illumination over a shorter distance.

## What are the disadvantages of a 25-50 degree beam angle?
One of the main disadvantages of a 25-50 degree beam angle is that it may not provide the same level of precision and control as a narrower beam angle. This can result in a less focused light beam, which can be detrimental to certain applications.
