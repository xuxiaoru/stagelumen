OEM is the cheapest way to put your own brand on a product line, and it is also the easiest way to end up with ninety boxes nobody can sell. The difference is not the factory — it is whether you specified the order properly.

## The short answer

OEM puts your brand on a product that already exists: same tooling, same certification, same firmware, new silkscreen, manual and carton. That is why it is fast and cheap. Expect a median minimum order quantity of 30 pieces per model across our range, confirm that the certification file carries over under your brand, and treat the packaging artwork as carefully as you treat the fixture sample.

## What does OEM actually change, and what stays the same?

Very little of the product. That is the point, and it is also what limits it.

| Element | OEM | ODM |
|---|---|---|
| Product design | Existing | Designed to your brief |
| Tooling | Existing | New tooling, paid for |
| Certification | Existing file, your brand added | New certification required |
| Firmware and DMX personality | Standard | Can be customised |
| Housing colour and finish | Selected from options | Custom |
| Logo, manual, carton | Yours | Yours |
| Cost | Catalogue price plus brand set-up | Higher, plus NRE |
| Lead time | Shortest | Longest |

If the goal is to get a branded line to market in one season, OEM is the answer. If the goal is a product nobody else sells, that is ODM, and it costs more and takes longer.

## What are the six terms worth negotiating?

| Term | What to pin down | Why it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| MOQ per model | Exact number, and whether mixed containers are allowed | "30 pieces" can mean 30 per model or 30 per order |
| Certification | Which file number, and whether your brand is named on it | A CE mark is only valid with the file behind it |
| Brand elements | Silkscreen, label, manual, carton print — all listed | Items get missed off the quotation and added later |
| Spare parts | Availability window and price list | Repairs become impossible when the run ends |
| Warranty scope | What is covered, who pays freight | Cross-border freight decides whether a claim is worth making |
| Sample approval | One production sample before the run | Without it, the first you see of the run is the container |

## How should a first OEM order be structured?

Small, mixed, and on a model you already know sells. A first order is a test of the process, not of the product, and the cheapest way to run that test is three models at the minimum quantity each.

| Order shape | Quantity | When it makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| Single model, minimum | 30 | You already know the model sells in your market |
| Three models, minimum each | 90 total | First OEM order — tests the process across the range |
| One model, volume tier | 50–100 | Established line, reordering |
| Mixed container | Negotiated | You are filling a container anyway |

A three-model order is the one we recommend for a first run. It puts three products on your catalogue at once, it spreads the risk if one model underperforms, and it exposes any weakness in the artwork and packaging process while the quantities are still small enough to absorb.

## What goes wrong, and how do you prevent it?

Packaging, manuals and labels — never the fixture. The product is a known quantity; the brand elements are new every time.

| Failure | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Misspelt model number on every carton | Approve a photographed production sample of the carton, not a PDF |
| Manual in the wrong language | Specify language and part number per market |
| Certification mark on the box but not in the file | Ask for the certificate number and check the model appears on it |
| Barcodes that do not scan | Specify the barcode format and test the printed sample |
| Brand label peeling | Ask what adhesive is used and on what surface |
| Colour mismatch between batches | Approve a physical colour chip, reference it in the order |

Every item on that list is preventable with a photograph. Ask for one before the run starts.
