Compliance

Scenario 01 — Large Format

Concert & Festival

High-output rigs built for long throw distances, fast cue changes and, when the stage is outdoors, full IP65 weather protection. We size the beam angles and fixture count to your actual trim height and throw distance — not to a generic package.

Stadium Arena Open-air festival Touring concert rig

Common challenges

  • Throw distance. A beam that looks sharp at 8 m turns to mush at 40 m. Wide beam angles simply cannot hold a defined shaft across a stadium.
  • Ambient light. Dusk sets and daylight festival slots wash out low-output fixtures, killing contrast before the show even starts.
  • Programming speed. Dozens of cues per song demand fast pan/tilt and instant colour changes — slow mechanics cost you the look.
  • Fleet scale. Addressing, firmware and fault-finding across 100+ fixtures is where most rigs lose hours.

Our approach

  • Narrow-beam optics. SL-B280 / B400 / B600 deliver 2°–4° beams with high centre intensity, holding a solid shaft well past 40 m.
  • Headroom for daylight. 600 W and 800 W flagships (SL-B600, SL-H800) for large stages and high ambient light.
  • Fast mechanics. Three-phase motors drive full 540°/270° travel in about 2.2 s with 16-bit positioning accuracy.
  • Network control. SL-C2 outputs Art-Net with RDM — batch-address the whole rig and read back fixture status from FOH.

Scenario 02 — Fleet Operations

Rental & Touring

Fixtures that survive hundreds of load-ins. We design for the service technician, not just the lighting designer: modular internals, standardised spares, unified firmware and colour consistency across every batch you own.

Rental houses Touring productions Road shows Corporate roadshows

Common challenges

  • Load-in wear. Housings, connectors and fans fail first when a fixture is trucked and rigged dozens of times a year.
  • Venue variability. One night a 400-seat hall, the next a 4,000-cap arena — you cannot carry a different fixture for each.
  • Downtime cost. A dead fixture mid-show is far more expensive than the fixture itself.
  • Fleet consistency. Mixed firmware and colour bins across batches make matching looks a nightmare.

Our approach

  • Modular service design. PSU, main board, LED module and fan are field-swappable — most repairs inside 10 minutes.
  • One fixture, three roles. The SL-H280 / H400 / H600 hybrids cover beam, spot and wash, cutting the number of SKUs you stock.
  • RDM diagnostics. Set addresses and read lamp hours, temperature and errors from the console — no ladder required.
  • Batch colour control. LED bins are graded per production batch so fixtures you buy next year still match this year's.

Scenario 03 — Precision Optics

Theatre & Performance

Theatre lighting is judged on what the camera and the audience do not notice: no flicker, no fan noise, no colour shift on skin, no stepping in a 30-second fade. Our Fresnel, profile and follow-spot range is specified around exactly those four constraints.

Proscenium theatre Black box Opera house Performing arts centre

Common challenges

  • Camera capture. Low PWM frequency produces banding and scan lines the moment you stream or record.
  • Skin tones. Low-CRI sources flatten faces and make costumes read wrong — the single most common complaint in houses of worship and theatre.
  • Ambient noise. In a quiet auditorium, fan noise is audible from the back row.
  • Long fades. 8-bit dimming visibly steps during slow cross-fades. Theatre needs 16-bit plus a proper curve.

Our approach

  • Flicker-free drivers. PWM ≥ 3 kHz across the range, safe for 4K capture and high frame rates.
  • High rendering. CRI ≥ 95 and TLCI ≥ 90 on the Fresnel and profile range for natural skin and costume colour.
  • Quiet operation. Temperature-controlled fan curves hold under 28 dB(A) at 1 m in silent mode.
  • Smooth dimming. 16-bit resolution with selectable linear, square and S-curve dimmer laws.

Scenario 04 — High Duty Cycle

Club & DJ

Low ceilings, ten-hour nights and an operator whose job is playing music, not running a console. We specify compact fixtures with built-in sound-active and auto modes, plus thermal management that keeps output stable all night.

Nightclub Cocktail bar DJ booth Live house

Common challenges

  • Low trim heights. At 3–5 m ceiling height, full-size moving heads simply do not fit.
  • Continuous runtime. Six to ten hours every night accelerates lumen depreciation and fan failure.
  • Non-specialist operation. The DJ needs the room to look good without programming a single cue.
  • Access cost. Fixtures are usually grid- or truss-mounted, so every service call is expensive.

Our approach

  • Compact bodies. 150–200 W models such as SL-B150 and SL-S200 are built for tight trim heights.
  • Standalone intelligence. Built-in sound-active, auto-run and master-slave modes — no console required.
  • Thermal protection. Smart fan curves and automatic output limiting protect LEDs during long shifts.
  • Dual control. DMX plus IR remote so a technician can fine-tune quickly on site.

Scenario 05 — Broadcast Ready

Church & Worship

Most congregations now stream every service, which turns the sanctuary into a television studio. That changes the specification: flicker-free output, high CRI on the speaker's face, and fans quiet enough to forget about.

Church Worship centre Cathedral Multi-purpose hall

Common challenges

  • Livestream quality. Camera sensors expose flicker and colour casts that the naked eye never sees in the room.
  • Facial rendering. The speaker must be evenly lit, shadow-free and natural — hard light and low CRI both fail here.
  • Silence. A quiet sanctuary makes fan noise obvious during prayer and spoken sections.
  • Volunteer operators. The system is usually run by volunteers with limited training and limited budget.

Our approach

  • Tunable white. CRI ≥ 95 with 2700 K–6500 K adjustment so front light matches house light and camera balance.
  • Broadcast-safe drivers. Flicker-free output across the dimming range — no banding on stream.
  • Silent operation. Under 28 dB(A), with fanless options for the most sensitive positions.
  • Scene presets. We ship pre-programmed scenes and run a 30-minute training session for your volunteers.

Scenario 06 — Fast Turnaround

Event & Wedding

No power where you need it and two hours to turn the room. Battery-powered wireless fixtures remove the cable run entirely, so a single technician can light a 300 m² ballroom without a single visible lead.

Wedding venue Banquet hall Corporate event Private party

Common challenges

  • No usable power. Heritage venues and marquees rarely have sockets where the design needs light.
  • Tight turnaround. Load-in to first dance is often two to four hours.
  • Visible cabling. Cables across a wedding aisle are neither safe nor photogenic.
  • Colour quality. Soft amber, blush and warm white — harsh RGB saturation looks cheap on camera.

Our approach

  • True wireless. SL-BPAR runs on internal lithium batteries with 2.4 GHz wireless DMX — zero cabling.
  • All-day runtime. 8–20 hours per charge, with charging flight cases for centralised fleet management.
  • Fast rigging. Clamp and magnetic mounts let one person place 20 fixtures in about 30 minutes.
  • Softer colour. RGBAW+UV six-colour engines (SL-PARWAUV) produce the pastel and amber tones weddings need.

Scenario 07 — Permanent Outdoor

Architectural & Outdoor

Once a fixture goes on a facade or a permanent mast, every service call becomes a costly access problem. Our IP65 range is built around sealed optics, corrosion-resistant housings and long-term colour stability.

Building facade Outdoor festival Theme park Permanent installation

Common challenges

  • Weather ingress. Rain, dust and salt spray destroy non-sealed fixtures within a season or two.
  • Thermal cycling. Day-to-night temperature swings drive condensation inside the housing.
  • Service access. A fixture 20 m up a facade costs far more to reach than it did to buy.
  • Long-term consistency. After three years, a replaced unit must still match its neighbours.

Our approach

  • Sealed IP65 optics. SL-IP-B400, SL-IP-W400 and SL-IP-H600 use sealed optical chambers rated for permanent outdoor use.
  • Corrosion resistance. Die-cast aluminium housings with UV-stable coating for coastal and high-sun exposure.
  • Condensation control. −20 °C to +45 °C operating range with a breather valve to equalise pressure.
  • Stable output. 50,000 h to L70, with LED bins held per batch so replacements match.
